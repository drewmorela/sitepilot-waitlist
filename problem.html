Plain-language framing

The problem, broken down

They want more qualified people engaging with their product every month while they keep building. A small software company already sells online and used to get trials or demos from the blog — but the founder stopped publishing because product work won. Hiring a writer is expensive. Skipping it means qualified organic engagement stops compounding.

What breaks is not “ideas.” It is steady, good-enough publishing on a schedule.

Store-window analogy

Google is a busy street. Your blog is the store window. Traffic keeps walking by. Competitors refresh their windows. You are busy running the shop, so yours stays empty.

SitePilot drafts and hangs new signs on a fixed schedule, after a quick check they are not junk, using the store’s existing window system (the CMS — the software that already holds and publishes your blog).

What that means for the product

Not a idea problem

Founders usually know what to write about. The failure mode is cadence.

Not “more AI words”

Unlimited spam fills the window with junk. The job is reliable scheduled publishing with a quality check.

Uses tools you have

Connect the CMS you already use. No new “write it somewhere else and paste it in” chore.
