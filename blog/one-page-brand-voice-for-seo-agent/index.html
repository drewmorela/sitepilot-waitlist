One-page brand voice notes an SEO publishing agent can actually follow

Small software teams ask an SEO publishing agent to “sound like us” and then hand over a 40-page brand book nobody reads. The agent improvises. The founder becomes the editor. Organic posts stall.

This article is a one-page voice brief you can fill in an afternoon and paste into every draft folder. It exists so a capped, quality-gated cadence can keep sending buyer-shaped readers while you keep building — without hiring a content team.

SitePilot’s bar matches the core promise: more qualified people engaging with their product every month while they keep building. Voice notes that an agent can execute beat poetry that only the founder understands.

Why one page beats a brand book

Brand-book problemOne-page voice fix
Too long to load into a draft briefFits above the outline in every markdown file
Vague adjectives (“bold, friendly, premium”)Concrete do / don’t rows
No fail rulesExplicit never-say + kill triggers
CTA chaosOne primary CTA pattern per post type
Proof theaterHonest scrap rules only

If a rule does not change a sentence the agent would write tomorrow, leave it off the page.

The one-page template (copy this)

Fill the blanks. Keep it under ~400 words total.

1) Who we write for (one sentence)

Founders / operators at small software companies who need [job] without [pain they refuse].

Example SitePilot-shaped line: founders who need a 2-post week of QA-gated SEO posts without becoming the editor.

2) Promise we may repeat

One sentence. No rankings guarantees. Align to product reality.

3) Tone dials (pick positions)

DialLeftRightOur setting
FormalityCasualFormal_______
HumorDry / nonePlayful_______
CertaintyHedgedDirect_______
LengthShort paragraphsLong essaysShort paragraphs
JargonPlainInsiderPlain + define once

Agents follow settings, not vibes.

4) Always do

  • Lead with the buyer decision, not the product feature dump.
  • Prefer tables, checklists, and numbered steps over metaphors.
  • Link one sibling post that answers the next question (see internal links that move buyers).
  • Put the product CTA once, in context, near the end.
  • Fail closed on claims — if you cannot source it, cut it (customer-evidence snippets).

5) Never say (fail-closed list)

Write the literal phrases that must not appear:

Never say / doWhy
Invented % uplift, “#1 on Google,” fake logosTrust tax; QA kill
“As an AI…” / filler throat-clearingBrand noise
Unlimited content / firehose promisesBreaks cadence cap
Guaranteed rankings or trafficUnknowable; dishonest
Competitor insults by nameOff-brand + legal risk
Urgency theater (“only 3 spots”) unless trueFake scarcity

Add your own product-specific bans (pricing jokes you hate, words investors misuse, etc.).

6) Proof rules

AllowedBanned
Anonymized quotes, ticket themes, changelog notesFabricated case studies
Product artifacts (checklists, UI paths)Waitlist or traffic counts you did not count
“We shipped X because users hit Y”Softened lies (“leading teams report…”)

7) CTA pattern

One pattern for method posts:

If you want [outcome] without [pain], [waitlist / trial / docs link] is the next step when you are ready.

No second soft CTA in the intro. No “subscribe for daily tips” if you do not send them.

8) Approve expectation

State how the founder approves (PR, reply approve <slug>, CMS OK). Reminder: no approve → no publish — same spine as approve without becoming editor.

How the agent uses the page

  1. Paste the filled page into the draft brief or a VOICE.md next to pipeline/drafts/.
  2. Check never-say before QA. A single banned phrase → rewrite once or kill the draft (fail-closed; do not soften banned claims into vague copy).
  3. Score brand_fit honestly. If the draft could belong to any SaaS blog, it fails brand fit even if grammar is fine — same idea as QA gates before you publish.
  4. Do not expand the page mid-week. Queue voice changes for Monday; mid-week edits create inconsistent posts inside the same 2-post cadence.

Example filled page (SitePilot dogfood shape)

Use as a model, not a copy-paste for your product:

  • Who: Founders of small software companies who need buyer-shaped organic posts without a content hire.
  • Promise: More qualified people engaging with the product every month while they keep building.
  • Tone: Direct, short paragraphs, dry humor rare, plain language.
  • Always: Tables · fail-closed · one sibling link · one contextual CTA · no invented metrics.
  • Never: Ranking guarantees · fake case studies · firehose volume · Idea Lab / ads-management claims.
  • CTA: Waitlist when ready; no invented signup totals.
  • Approve: Founder (or CEO agent under board rules) records approved_by + approved_at before publish.

That is enough for an agent to draft inside a two-post week without guessing your personality from old tweets.

Fit inside the cluster

Brand voice is ops infrastructure, not a vanity About page. It supports:

  • Cadence posts that stay on-promise
  • QA brand_fit that means something
  • Faster approve cycles
  • Safer evidence and CTA habits

Ship the voice page once, then let method posts link back when founders ask “how do we keep sounding like us?” Do not invent a separate “brand storytelling” cluster until the cadence cluster is solid.

Recap

  • One page beats a brand book: dials, always/never, proof, CTA, approve.
  • Agents execute settings and fail-closed lists, not vibes.
  • Check never-say before QA; kill or rewrite on banned claims.
  • Keep the page stable inside a week; change it on Monday with the digest.
  • Voice exists to protect qualified engagement, not to decorate thin drafts.
