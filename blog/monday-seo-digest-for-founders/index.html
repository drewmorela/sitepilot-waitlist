The Monday SEO digest: what a founder should see every week

Cadence without visibility decays into hope. If you run a capped SEO schedule for a small software company, the founder should get a short Monday digest — not a 20-page analytics PDF and not a Slack wall of “we published something.”

This is the report format SitePilot uses on dogfood and aims to deliver to beta customers: what shipped, what failed a gate, what was skipped, and what’s next. No invented traffic. No vanity rank screenshots.

Why Monday, not “whenever”

Pick one weekday and keep it. Monday PT (or the customer’s preferred day) works because:

  • Last week’s posts are live and linkable.
  • This week’s outlines can still change before draft day.
  • One recurring email (or markdown file) beats ad-hoc status pings.

If nothing shipped, the digest still goes out. Empty weeks are information: either quality held, approval stalled, or the queue needs a new cluster.

Section 1 — Shipped this week

List every public URL that actually went live. Include title, URL, and who approved.

FieldWhy it matters
TitleReminds the founder what the cluster is about
Live URLProof of publish path — not “draft somewhere”
Approved byMakes edit/approve real, not theater

If a post is still waiting on merge or CMS publish, label it draft-only. Do not pad the shipped list with local files.

For how those posts fit a weekly rhythm, see The 2-post week.

Section 2 — QA summary

Founders do not need a lecture on process. They need counts:

  • Published with all gates pass
  • Fail → rewrite before publish
  • Killed drafts

Gates that matter: originality, structure, readability, brand fit, anti-spam, internal links, plus explicit approve. Detail the checklist in What QA gates should catch before you hit publish — the digest only needs the scoreboard.

Rule: if something failed and still shipped, the digest is lying. Fix the pipeline, not the report.

Section 3 — Skipped slots

Skipping is allowed. Document why:

  • Quality hold (failed originality or niche fit)
  • Waiting on customer approve
  • Cadence cap (already hit N/week)

A skipped slot with a reason beats a thin post that dilutes the site. Treat skip as a healthy signal when the bar is real.

Section 4 — Next queue

List the next 2–3 titles with target publish dates. Keep them inside one topic cluster. If the queue is empty, say so and ask for a cluster sentence: who buys, what job, which product surface.

Do not invent next week’s titles from a keyword dump. Prefer titles a buyer would bookmark.

Section 5 — One clear ask

End with a single customer action when blocked:

  • Approve PR / reply “approve &lt;slug&gt;”
  • Confirm CMS credentials or repo access
  • Pick between two cluster options
  • Confirm preferred digest weekday

Never stack five asks. One unblock per week compounds faster.

What not to put in the digest

Tempting fillerWhy skip it
Fake “traffic up 40%”Too early / often wrong; kills trust
Competitor screenshotsNot the job of a cadence report
Unlimited idea listsDilutes the capped queue
Tool cheerleadingFounders want URLs and next steps

Honest baseline: when rankings or sessions exist later, report them with dates and caveats. Until then, ship URLs and QA truth.

  • Title (target date)
  • Title (target date)

    • ```

That is enough for a founder to skim in under three minutes while they keep building product.
