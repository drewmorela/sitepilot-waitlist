What “approve without becoming the editor” looks like in week 3

Week one of a new content cadence is easy to approve. Everything is novel, the founder reads every sentence, and the first two posts get more attention than they will ever need again. Week two is still a little careful. Week three is where the real habit shows up, and where most small software teams quietly slide back into editing.

The approve-without-becoming-editor flow is simple on paper: agents draft, gates fail closed, a named person says approve, edit-then-approve, or kill. This post is about what that flow should feel like once the novelty is gone. It serves the same promise as everything else here: more qualified people engaging with the product every month while the team keeps building. A review loop that keeps shrinking is how the "keep building" part stays true.

The week-3 shape: shorter, not stricter

By week three the review should look different from week one in three specific ways.

Week 1Week 3
Founder reads every paragraphFounder reads the opening, every H2, and one sample section
Fix list has style notes ("tighten this", "less formal")Fix list is empty or has one factual correction
Approval arrives after a day or two of thinkingApproval arrives inside the same working session
Edits happen inside the draftEdits happen in the brief, before the next draft exists

The last row matters most. If a correction keeps showing up in the draft, it belongs one level up. Move it into the one-page brand voice brief or the cluster sentence, and the next draft arrives without it.

None of this means lowering the bar. The QA gates still run on every draft. The founder is just no longer the gate that catches what the gates should have caught.

Three signals you are drifting back into editor mode

Drift is quiet. Nobody decides to become the editor again. It happens one "small fix" at a time. Watch for these in week three:

  1. Your fix list has the same note twice. "Too salesy in the intro" on two drafts in a row means the brief is wrong, not the drafts. Fix the brief once and stop writing the note.
  2. You are rewriting openings. The opening sets the frame for a buyer. If you keep rewriting it, the cluster sentence is probably fuzzy. Run the cluster sentence stress test before the next draft.
  3. Approval waits for a free evening. If a draft sits until you can "give it proper attention", review has grown past the ten-minute script. That is a pipeline problem showing up as a calendar problem.

Each of these has the same fix: push the correction upstream, then go back to skimming.

A week-3 approval, start to finish

Here is an illustrative example of what a healthy week-three review looks like for a two-post week. It is a pattern, not a customer quote.

  • Monday morning. The Monday digest lists two queued drafts, both QA PASS. The founder opens the first one between standup and a product call.
  • Skim. Title matches the buyer job. Opening is fine. H2s read like a buyer's questions. One sample section checked. Internal links click through.
  • One note. A paragraph mentions an integration that is still on the roadmap. The founder replies: "edit: remove the roadmap mention; then approve."
  • Done. Under ten minutes. The agent removes the line, re-runs QA, publishes, and adds a rule to the brief: no unreleased features in drafts.
  • Tuesday. The second draft arrives without any roadmap mention. Approve, no edits.

Notice where the effort went. The one real correction turned into a brief rule, so Tuesday cost less than Monday. That is the compounding you want by week three.

When the right answer is kill or skip

Week three is also when the first genuinely weak draft tends to show up. The topic queue has burned through its most obvious titles, and the next one is thinner. The temptation is to fix it, because the streak feels fragile.

Do not fix it. Use the same three replies you always had:

SituationReplyWhy
Draft is solid, one factual erroredit-then-approveSmall, specific, and done in one pass
Draft is off-niche or wrong buyerkillKilling beats a rewrite loop and frees the slot
Nothing in the queue is good enough this weekskip the slotSkipping keeps the habit without diluting the blog

A skipped slot in week three is not a failure. A post you would not send to a customer is.

What to change in the pipeline after week 3

At the end of week three, spend ten minutes on the system instead of the drafts:

  1. Read your three weeks of fix notes together. Any note that appears more than once becomes a brief rule.
  2. Check the queue against the cluster sentence. Kill any title that needs a "for example" to connect.
  3. Check review time. If it is not trending down, one of the drift signals above is active. Name it.
  4. Confirm the internal links still work. By week three there are enough siblings that reciprocal links should be routine, not a chore.
  5. Write one line in the Monday digest about what changed, so the next review starts from the new bar.

That is the whole retrospective. No dashboard, no meeting.

Recap

  • By week three, review should be shorter, not stricter.
  • A repeated fix note is a brief problem, not a draft problem.
  • Rewriting openings or waiting for a free evening means you are drifting back into editor mode.
  • Turn each real correction into a brief rule so the next draft arrives clean.
  • Kill or skip weak drafts instead of fixing them to protect a streak.

If approval still takes an evening in week three, fix the pipeline, not the prose.
