What QA gates should catch before you hit publish

A fail-closed SEO QA checklist for small SaaS blogs: originality, structure, brand fit, anti-spam, internal links, and explicit approve.

Most “AI blog” damage is not one bad sentence. It is shipping without a fail-closed gate: near-duplicate intros, keyword walls, no path to the product, and no human who said “yes, this can go live.”

If you sell software to other businesses and your blog used to convert trials, quality gates are how you protect that channel while you regain cadence. This is the checklist SitePilot runs before publish — usable even if you run it as a founder with a markdown folder.

Gate 1 — Originality (rewrite or kill)

Ask: would a careful reader think we pasted this from a generic roundup?

Pass when claims are specific to your ICP, examples are yours, and the draft is not a near-duplicate of a prior post. Fail when the opening could sit on any SaaS blog unchanged, or when paragraphs echo known public posts. On fail: rewrite the hollow sections or kill the slot. Do not “lightly paraphrase” and ship.

Gate 2 — Structure (H1 + H2s, scannable)

Pass when there is a clear H1, multiple H2s a founder can skim in under three minutes, and no wall-of-text. Prefer short sections, lists where they clarify steps, and one idea per heading. Fail when the piece is a single blob, headings are keyword stuffing, or the outline was never written.

If the outline was mush, the draft will be mush — fix the outline first. For how that fits a weekly rhythm, see The 2-post week.

Gate 3 — Readability (concrete steps over fluff)

Pass when a busy founder can extract an action without rereading. Prefer “do X on Monday, Y before publish” over abstractions about “leveraging content.” Cut throat-clearing intros. Fail when every paragraph could be deleted without changing what the reader should do.

Gate 4 — Brand / niche fit

Pass when the article belongs on your site for your buyers. A post about generic “AI productivity hacks” on a vertical SaaS blog is bait, not SEO. Fail when the topic only exists to chase volume. Kill or re-topic — do not publish off-niche filler to “keep the streak.”

Gate 5 — Anti-spam (cap + density)

Pass when cadence stays inside the weekly cap (SitePilot default: 2/week) and the draft is not stuffed with repeated target phrases. Fail when the plan is unlimited pages, or when the same keyword appears so often a human winces. Skip the slot rather than dilute the site.

Gate 6 — Internal links (at least one real one)

Pass when the draft includes ≥1 relevant link to a product surface or a related guide — planned in the outline, present in the body. Example: pointing readers at the SitePilot waitlist when the topic is scheduled SEO publishing for small software teams. Fail when the URL is an orphan, or the only “link” is a placeholder. Add the link before publish; do not defer forever.

Gate 7 — Explicit approve

Pass when someone accountable records approve (PR approve, “approve <slug>” reply, or dogfood sign-off with timestamp). Fail when the pipeline auto-publishes from draft. Light customer edits are expected; incorporate them, then ship.

Gate 8 — Publish path + URL recorded

Pass when the file lands in the correct repo path (or CMS status), the live URL is known, and the weekly report can list it. Fail when “published” means a local draft nobody can open. Fix path, then retry.

How to run this without theater

Store results next to the draft (frontmatter or a short qa note). Example fields: originality, structure, readability, brand_fit, anti_spam, internal_links, approved_by, approved_at. Any fail → rewrite or kill; never publish on fail.

A week with one strong post and one skipped slot beats two weak posts. That is the quality bar behind SitePilot: scheduled, quality-gated SEO publishing so more qualified people can engage with the product every month — while the team keeps building.
