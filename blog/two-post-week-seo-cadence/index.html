The 2-post week: how small SaaS teams keep SEO without a content hire

If you run a small SaaS product, you already know the pattern: three strong blog posts after a launch, then silence for a month while roadmap work wins. Search does not reward hero weeks. It rewards showing up on a schedule with pages that help a buyer finish a job.

You do not need a content department for that. You need a capped cadence, a single topic cluster, and a fail-closed quality bar. Two posts per week is enough to compound and small enough that a founder (or an agent pipeline) can keep it when shipping is hard.

Why two beats “whenever we can”

One post a month feels responsible and dies the first busy sprint. Unlimited AI publishing feels productive and floods the site with thin pages nobody links to. Two posts per week sits in the middle:

  • Predictable ops. Monday outline, Tuesday–Wednesday draft, Thursday QA + edit, Friday publish (or split across two ship days).
  • Cluster depth. Eight posts a month is enough to cover one niche thoroughly instead of spraying twenty disconnected keywords.
  • Skip is allowed. If a draft fails originality or brand fit, you skip the slot. Empty is better than spam.

Treat the number as a cap, not a quota to fill with mush.

Pick one cluster, not a keyword spreadsheet

Before you write anything, write one sentence: who buys, what job they are trying to finish, and which product surface that job touches. Example: “B2B SaaS founders who need onboarding emails that raise activation without hiring lifecycle marketing.”

From that sentence, list 4–6 related titles that a real buyer would search or bookmark:

  1. Checklist for week-one onboarding emails
  2. How to measure activation without vanity opens
  3. When to use in-app vs email for the same nudge
  4. Common copy mistakes that stall trials
  5. How to recycle one teardown into three posts

Publish inside that list until it is exhausted. Then pick the next cluster. This is how SitePilot scopes work for customers: cadence plus niche fit, not a firehose.

A weekly loop you can keep on a shipping week

  1. Monday — topic lock. Choose the next title from the cluster queue. Write the H1, three H2s, the reader’s job-to-be-done, and one planned internal link (product page or sibling guide).
  2. Draft day — constraints on. Cap length to what a busy founder finishes in under three minutes of skimming. Prefer steps, tables, and examples over “AI is changing content” filler.
  3. QA before anyone sees it. Run structure, readability, niche fit, anti-spam, and internal-link checks. Fail closed: rewrite or kill.
  4. Edit / approve. A human (or dogfood sign-off) must explicitly approve. Light edits are expected; auto-publish is not.
  5. Publish + reciprocal links. Ship the post, then add links from related guides back to it when relevant.
  6. Friday — three-line report. What shipped, what failed a gate, what’s next. Cadence without visibility decays into hope.

If you want the gate list spelled out as a checklist, read What QA gates should catch before you hit publish.

Internal links are part of the draft, not a cleanup chore

Every article should leave with at least one useful link:

  • To a product or waitlist page when the topic naturally supports the offer
  • To a related guide in the same cluster

Links should help a reader finish the job. If you cannot name a natural link, the outline is probably too vague or off-niche — fix that before drafting.
